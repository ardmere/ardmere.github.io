# Product

## Register

product

## Users

ardmere serves regulators, policy researchers, institutional allocators, market makers, custody clients, independent reviewers, audit firms, exchange transparency teams, researchers, and media analysts. They arrive with a concrete risk question: whether an exchange's Proof-of-Reserves disclosure is effective, what evidence is missing, and whether public claims such as "audited", "ZK verified", or "100% backed" are over-stated.

These users usually do not want to run proofs manually on first visit. They need a credible front door into ratings, reports, verifier evidence, artifact hashes, missing evidence, and upgrade paths.

## Product Purpose

ardmere is PoR transparency intelligence infrastructure. It helps external observers determine whether an exchange's PoR is publicly verifiable, whether it meets the minimum effective PoR standard, and which artifacts or proof constraints are missing.

Success means a visitor can quickly understand three things: Stage 0 is not effective PoR, Stage 1 is the minimum effective PoR threshold, and every public rating is bound to artifacts, hashes, verifier outputs, timestamps, and explicit `UNVERIFIABLE` gaps.

## Brand Personality

Institutional, cryptographic, independent.

The voice should feel precise, evidence-bound, and calm under scrutiny. It should avoid hype and avoid looking like an exchange marketing page. The interface should project public-interest rigor: closer to a risk intelligence terminal or audit evidence index than a generic Web3 landing page.

## Anti-references

- Do not look like a cyberpunk hacker collective, DEF CON splash page, or Matrix demo.
- Do not use decorative terminal effects, glitch text, fake live feeds, or simulated chain activity as the primary identity.
- Do not look like exchange marketing or a paid certification badge.
- Do not bury the comparison table behind abstract brand storytelling.
- Do not use generic SaaS card grids, purple gradients, glassmorphism, or decorative proof imagery that is not tied to real evidence.

## Design Principles

1. Lead with the verdict, then show the evidence trail.
2. Treat missing data as first-class information, not fine print.
3. Make methodology legible before asking users to trust ratings.
4. Separate public independent ratings from paid technical assessments.
5. Use restraint to create trust; use visual emphasis only for Stage, risk, and verification state.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA contrast for all text and controls. Support reduced motion. Do not rely on color alone for Stage, pass/fail, or risk state. Keep report links, evidence labels, and risk flags readable for non-technical institutional users while preserving exact technical terminology where it affects methodology.
